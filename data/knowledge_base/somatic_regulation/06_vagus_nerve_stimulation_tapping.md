## 2. Summary & Operational Objective
An optional tactile sensory grounding practice involving rhythmic, alternating touch (often known as the 'butterfly tap'). 

Offered purely as a non-clinical sensory refocusing exercise that some individuals find calming, not as an accredited psychotherapy, trauma recovery intervention, or physiological vagal treatment.

---

## 3. Step-by-Step Instructions & Protocol Details
1. Sit in a comfortable, supported chair with an uncrossed, relaxed posture.
2. Cross arms over the chest, resting hands lightly near the collarbones or upper arms.
3. Tap gently and rhythmically, alternating between left and right hands at a steady, unhurried pace (roughly 1 tap per second).
4. Direct attention toward the physical sensation of the fingers making contact, maintaining slow, natural breathing.
5. Continue for 1 to 2 minutes if comfortable. Stop whenever ready.

---

## 4. Clinical Limitations & Boundaries
> [!WARNING]
> ### Caution & Discontinuation
> - **Unverified Clinical Claims:** Cutaneous tapping does not stimulate the vagus nerve in a clinically verified manner, nor does it resolve traumatic memories or clinical panic.
> - **Discomfort or Agitation:** For some individuals, repetitive self-touch or somatic focus can trigger emotional unease, agitation, or sensory irritation. Discontinue immediately if any distress arises.
> - **Non-Treatment:** This is a subjective coping aid. It must never substitute for evidence-based psychotherapy (such as EMDR or Trauma-Focused CBT) provided by licensed practitioners.
