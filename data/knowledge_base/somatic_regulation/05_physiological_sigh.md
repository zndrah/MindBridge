## 2. Summary & Operational Objective
An evidence-informed, optional breathing practice consisting of a two-step inhalation followed by an extended exhalation. Evaluated in clinical psychophysiology studies (Balban et al., 2023) for transient reduction in subjective tension and autonomic arousal.

Offered as a voluntary coping tool during elevated stress, not as a guaranteed panic interruption or medical cure.

---

## 3. Step-by-Step Instructions & Protocol Details
1. Inhale gently through the nose to fill roughly 80% of lung capacity.
2. At the top of the inhale, take a brief, unforced second sip of air through the nose to fully expand the chest.
3. Exhale slowly and completely through an open mouth with an unforced sigh, letting the shoulders drop.
4. Repeat 2 to 3 times in a slow, natural rhythm. Return to ordinary breathing afterward.

---

## 4. Clinical Limitations & Boundaries
> [!WARNING]
> ### Caution & Discontinuation
> - **Dizziness & Discomfort:** Hyperventilation can occur if breaths are repeated too quickly. If you experience dizziness, lightheadedness, or discomfort, stop immediately and allow your breathing to normalize.
> - **No Guaranteed Cure:** Respiration exercises do not guarantee immediate panic reduction and work differently across individuals.
> - **Medical Exclusion:** Do not use this exercise as a substitute for emergency medical care during acute asthma attacks, chest pain, or true medical respiratory distress.
