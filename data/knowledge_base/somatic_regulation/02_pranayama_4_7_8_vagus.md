## 2. Summary & Operational Objective
An evidence-informed, optional breathing exercise utilizing extended exhalations to facilitate subjective relaxation and perceived calm during non-crisis stress or evening winding down. 

This technique is offered as a voluntary coping tool, not as a guaranteed panic intervention or medical cure.

---

## 3. Step-by-Step Instructions & Protocol Details
1. Sit comfortably with an upright posture and feet flat on the floor.
2. Rest the tip of the tongue gently against the tissue ridge behind the upper front teeth.
3. Inhale quietly through the nose for a count of 4 seconds.
4. Hold the breath comfortably for a count of 7 seconds. (*Note: If holding causes strain or air hunger, shorten or omit the pause.*)
5. Exhale gently and steadily through the mouth for a count of 8 seconds.
6. Complete 2 to 4 cycles initially. Return to natural breathing afterward.

---

## 4. Clinical Limitations & Boundaries
> [!WARNING]
> ### Caution & Discontinuation
> - **Dizziness & Discomfort:** Paced breathing or breath retention can cause mild hyperventilation, dizziness, or tingling in some users. If you feel lightheaded, uneasy, or short of breath, stop immediately and resume your normal breathing rhythm.
> - **Non-Treatment:** This exercise is a supportive self-regulation tool. It does not treat clinical anxiety disorders, panic disorders, or respiratory illness.
> - **Physical Safety:** Always practice while seated or resting. Never practice while driving, bathing, or operating machinery.
